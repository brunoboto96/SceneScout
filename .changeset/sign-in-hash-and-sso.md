With a saved sign-in, a sign-in page is now recognised in two more places. A hash-routed app's sign-in route (`/#/login`, `/#!/auth/sign-in`) counts as a bounce, for a navigation and for the check each later call makes; `/#/loginhelp` and an in-page `#login` anchor still do not. A page sent to a single sign-on provider's sign-in on another origin counts too: an address with a sign-in segment, or a provider endpoint (`/authorize`, `/oauth2/…`, `/saml2/…`, `/sso/…`, `/adfs/…`, `/openid-connect/…`) carrying a sign-in request's parameters (`client_id`, `redirect_uri`, `SAMLRequest` and the like). A navigation the app sends straight to the provider is judged by where it went, not the app page the origin fence took it back to, so the route is no longer counted as covered. A move to an ordinary page of another site is not a bounce. After an action of the tester's own lands on a sign-in page (a click on Sign out), the pages that then send the session to sign-in are still recorded as not covered, but no longer raise `⚠ SESSION AUTH LOST` or re-attach a role session, until the session signs in again (an action from a sign-in page into the app, or a page that bounced since reached signed in) or a new attach starts. A sign-out explains at most three different routes; a fourth counts toward the verdict with them. A back to a sign-in page is not a sign-out. An action whose request the app answered 401 before it went to sign-in is a sign-in that ran out, not a sign-out, and later bounces count as before.
